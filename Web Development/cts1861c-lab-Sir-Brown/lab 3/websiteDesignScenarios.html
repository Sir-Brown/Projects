Website Design Scenarios

Problem Scenario:

A small business owner, Samantha, is struggling to find a reliable web development and graphic design service to establish her brand's online presence. She has browsed multiple websites, but most are either too expensive, lack clear service descriptions, or don't provide a seamless way to request a quote. She tries one company, but their website is slow and difficult to navigate. She tries another, but the cost is too high. This leaves her without a website to showcase her products and services.

Activity Scenario:

Samantha stumbles upon another website (our site) giving it one last go. She is welcomed by a clean, modern, and easy-to-navigate homepage. She clicks on the "Services" page and browses the different options. She selects "Graphic Design" and finds details on logo creation, branding, and print design. She then clicks on the "Get a Quote" button, fills in a short form, and submits her request. The site provides her three free previews of what her logo could look like. Within a few minutes, a confirmation email containing a timeframe for a response, is sent out. Impressed by the company's professionalism, she considers hiring them to create her website as well.

Information Design Scenario:

The main object for the activity scenario above would include the website, which would include the homepage, services page, quote form, and confirmation email. The actions involved would be Samantha browsing the homepage, selecting the services page, selecting a service, filling out the quote form, the webpage generating free previews as examples and then issuing a confirmation email.

Interaction Design Scenario:

With the same activity scenario in mind, this would include smooth transitions between pages, minimalist design to not overstimulate users, clear and concise service descriptions, a user-friendly quote form with client-side verification, and a confirmation email that is sent out immediately after the form is submitted. The website should be easy to navigate and provide a seamless experience for the user. It should also feel familiar to "prevent the user from thinking".

Protyping and Refinement:

Does the combination of these scenarios create a strong and consistent guideline for the website's analysis and design phases?
Yes. The problem scenario (analysis phase) defines the user's pain points, while the activity, information design, and interaction scenarios (design phase) build a roadmap for how the website should function, look, and feel based on lesson 2's usability specification.
